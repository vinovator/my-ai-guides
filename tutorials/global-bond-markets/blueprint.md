# The Essential Guide to Global Bond Markets

*How the largest market on earth works, why its interest rates become the price of money for everyone else, and how to read what it is telling you.*

---

## Why this guide exists

Bonds are the quietest important thing in finance. Shares get the headlines, but bonds are bigger, and the rates they produce sit underneath almost every other price in the economy: your mortgage, a company's borrowing costs, the value of the stock market, what a government can afford to spend.

At the end of 2025, bonds outstanding worldwide were worth about **$160.7 trillion**, a little more than the **$157.8 trillion** value of all the world's listed shares.

Most explanations of this market fail in one of two ways. They either stay so high-level that you learn vocabulary without mechanism, or they jump straight to the mathematics and lose you at the second equation. This guide takes a third route: it builds one mechanism at a time, shows you the arithmetic where the arithmetic is the point, and then puts each idea to work on a real episode where it decided the outcome.

> **The single most important idea in the subject:** a bond's price and its yield move in opposite directions. "Yields rose" and "bond prices fell" are the same headline written two ways. Lesson 1 takes that slowly, because everything else rests on it.

### What you will be able to do at the end

- Explain what a bond is, what its price and yield mean, and why they move against each other.
- Say how far a bond's price will move when yields change, and why long bonds move far more than short ones.
- Take any government bond yield apart into the pieces that built it.
- Read a yield curve, and know what an inversion does and does not tell you.
- Explain how a sell-off in New York raises a mortgage rate in London within days.
- Recognise the five risks inside any bond, and tell the difference between a bond and a bond fund.
- Decode an ordinary bond headline, and run a new one through five diagnostic questions.

### Who it is for

Anyone who reads financial news and wants the bond parts to stop being noise. No prior finance is assumed. There is no code, and the only arithmetic is arithmetic you can check on a calculator.

---

## How the guide is built

**Three markets throughout.** Rather than describe an abstract "bond market," the guide follows the **United States, United Kingdom and India** side by side. They are deliberately different: the US market is the world's benchmark safe asset, the UK is shaped by pension funds to an unusual degree, and India is a large domestic market just now opening to foreign money. Seeing the same mechanism play out three ways is what makes it general.

**Every concept gets an episode.** Duration is abstract until you watch it destroy a bank. Liquidity risk is abstract until six Indian funds shut their doors. Lesson 6 is eight such episodes, and each one isolates a different piece of the machinery.

**Read it in order.** Lessons 1 to 4 build the mechanism, Lesson 5 puts it in three real markets, Lesson 6 stress-tests it against history, and Lessons 7 to 9 turn it into something you can use.

## The lessons

### 1. [Anatomy and the Seesaw](lesson-01-anatomy-and-the-seesaw.md)

What a bond actually is, the six words you need (issuer, face value, coupon, maturity, price, yield), and then the central mechanism: why price and yield move in opposite directions, how far they move, and why a 30-year bond swings eight times harder than a 2-year. Introduces **duration** from first principles, both as the balance point of a bond's payments and as its price sensitivity, then **convexity**, with tables you can verify yourself.

### 2. [What Sets the Level of Yields](lesson-02-what-sets-the-level-of-yields.md)

Why a yield is 5% rather than 2% or 9%. Takes a government yield apart into expected policy rates and the term premium, adds inflation and the difference between nominal and real returns, then covers supply and demand, quantitative easing and tightening, and the credit spread that every non-government borrower pays. Ends with the yield curve and what an inversion is worth as a signal.

### 3. [Instruments, Players, and Plumbing](lesson-03-instruments-players-and-plumbing.md)

The market itself. The kinds of bonds that exist and how credit ratings set the price of borrowing; who the borrowers, lenders, dealers and referees are and what each of them wants; how new bonds are born at auction; why most bonds do not trade on an exchange; and the repo market, the overnight plumbing that finances everything and that nobody notices until it breaks.

### 4. [The Gravitational Pull](lesson-04-the-gravitational-pull.md)

Why any of this matters outside the bond market. Government yields as the base price of money: how they set mortgage and company borrowing costs, squeeze government budgets, re-value shares and property, and move currencies, pensions and banks. Also why the transmission works differently in India than in the US or UK.

### 5. [Three Markets Up Close: US, UK and India](lesson-05-three-markets-up-close.md)

Treasuries, gilts and G-secs in detail: who issues them, who buys them, how they trade, and what makes each market's character. A side-by-side comparison table, then September 2026 as a live worked example in which an energy shock runs through all three markets at once and nearly every concept from Lessons 1 to 4 shows up in the same month.

### 6. [Eight Headlines, Eight Lessons](lesson-06-eight-headlines-eight-lessons.md)

Eight episodes from the past two decades, each isolating one part of the machinery: Lehman and the failure of AAA (2008), America downgraded while its bonds rallied (2011), Greece and the currency you do not control (2010–12), the taper tantrum and the rupee (2013), Austria's 100-year bond (2020–23), Franklin Templeton's frozen funds (2020), the UK mini-budget and the LDI doom loop (2022), and "Sell America" (2025).

### 7. [Risks, and Bonds versus Bond Funds](lesson-07-risks-and-bond-funds.md)

The five risks inside every bond, which are interest-rate, credit, inflation, liquidity and currency, each matched to the episode where it did the damage. Then a distinction that catches a great many people: a bond matures and a bond fund never does, and what that means for whether losses eventually repair themselves.

### 8. [Reading the Market Yourself](lesson-08-reading-the-market-yourself.md)

The practical closer. A translation table for the jargon you will meet, five diagnostic questions that decode almost any bond story, the handful of ideas worth keeping if you forget everything else, and a three-layer reading list with a daily, weekly and quarterly routine for using it.

### 9. [The Whole Market in Pictures](lesson-09-visual-recap.md)

The guide restated as eighteen figures, one per idea, each captioned with what to notice and linked back to the lesson that taught it. Useful as revision, and as something to flick through before you next read the markets page.

## A note on the numbers

Every piece of arithmetic in this guide has been checked. The bond price table in Lesson 1, the duration figures, and the convexity asymmetry are all reproducible from the standard present-value formula, and you are encouraged to verify them.

One thing is worth flagging plainly: market data quoted as current reflects **September 2026**. Yields move daily, so treat those figures as a snapshot of a particular month rather than a live quote. The mechanisms they illustrate do not change when the numbers do, which is the reason the guide teaches the mechanism first and the number second.
